# ATS-Friendly Formatting Guidance

**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-24

These are default heuristics for a readable technical resume, not guarantees that a resume will be parsed correctly by every applicant tracking system.

## Layout and typography

- Prefer a clean, consistent, mostly single-column layout for the ATS-oriented version.
- Use familiar, readable fonts and a practical body size, commonly around 10–12 pt in typical document templates.
- Use consistent margins, spacing, date formats, headings and bullet indentation.
- Prefer text-based DOCX and PDF output. Validate that exported PDF text can be selected/extracted.
- Avoid relying on color, icons, images, graphics, sidebars, tables or text boxes to communicate essential information; some ATS parsers may not interpret these reliably.
- Make contact details and relevant links easy to locate. Use meaningful link text and a plain text URL when needed.
- Avoid complex headers/footers for content essential to parsing.
- Use standard section headings such as Experience, Skills, Education, Certifications and Projects where relevant.
- The one-page rule is not universal. Keep a resume concise; for an experienced technical candidate, one or two pages may be appropriate depending on relevant evidence and market. Epic Games' early-career guidance says one page is common but not an absolute rule and that there is rarely a need for more than two pages. Follow explicit employer instructions.

## Content structure

- Lead with the most relevant evidence for the target role.
- Choose section order based on the role and candidate experience, not a rigid universal template.
- For support/tooling/operations roles, technical skills and experience evidence may appear early if they quickly establish relevance.
- Keep employment dates, titles and organization names consistent and unambiguous.
- Use concise bullets with clear verbs and a factual task/action/result or impact where possible.
- Do not invent metrics or claim business impact without evidence.
- Avoid dense keyword lists that obscure demonstrated experience.

## Submission format

- Follow the application portal's explicit format instructions.
- Provide both DOCX and PDF when the workflow supports them, but use the format requested by the employer.
- Check the generated text and visual rendering before sending. A PDF may look correct while extracting in the wrong order; a DOCX may reflow on another system.
- Ensure links, punctuation, Unicode characters, page breaks and dates remain correct in each exported format.

## Caveats

- ATS products and employer workflows vary; no general checklist guarantees correct parsing or selection.
- Country conventions differ. Photo, personal details, page length and CV/resume terminology should not be hard-coded as universal rules.
- If the source file is a scan/image-only PDF or extraction warnings are high, ask the user for a text-based copy or manual correction; do not claim a complete analysis.
